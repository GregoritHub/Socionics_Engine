P0003 A complex is a core pattern of emotions, memories, perceptions, and wishes in the personal unconscious organized around a common theme. The locus of control, in its infancy, manifests the crux of its dilemma 
P0004 through the manifestation of light and dark beings which occupy the consciousness. The inner conflicts become expressions of external conflicts which are projected to the world and the self in the form of primitive models which we call archetypes. At the conscious level of the psyche, there are functional types of regulation that determine the general attitudes which may direct the archetypal expressions towards specific manifestations of valence in accordance with preferred stimulus.

P0033 Introduction
P0034 The Latin word ‘archetype’ is derived from the Greek words ‘arkhe’, which means ‘primitive’, and ‘tupos’ which means ‘model’. Combined, ‘arkhetupon’ is something which should be moulded first as a model.
P0036 Psychological archetypes are the primitive ‘god-figures’ which are prevalent in society. Their images are manifested in each individual and become the figurative narratives they use to define their relationships with the world. While there are a potentially infinite number of archetypal images, there are eight of them which have been identified to occupy the core narratives in every individual. Various names can be given to these archetypes but the semantic is only a symbol for the pragmatic force which binds the meaning to the actions which manifest from its discovery in either the self or in others.
P0037 The primitive models, as they are communicated, are subjective interpretations of the consciousness. The psychological archetype is the primitive model as it is modeled within the unconscious before it projects itself outwardly. The archetypes discussed here are considered to be collective images which exist in the minds of every human being regardless of origin. They are thought to be the primitive aspects of survival which have been evolved though our species since the original inception of life.

P0060 An Overview of Archetypal Images
P0061 The choice to use Abrahamic images is a personal bias of the author which is meant to provide a point of reference that can be accessed by any. Should a reader possess a personal bias against this choice of images, they are free to abstract the concepts however they wish. The meaning of the images is far more valuable than the context in which they are presented.
P0063 God created the Devil before all else, to shed light upon his creations and remove them from the shadows. He created Adam to be the caretaker over creation and he created Eve to be his helper.
P0064 In this myth, it is irrelevant which is masculine or feminine. Likewise, it is irrelevant which is good and which is evil. These concepts are not introduced until the fall from paradise. What is relevant is that Adam and Eve are both parent and child to one another and they are charged by God to be caretakers over an abundant paradise. God warns both Adam and Eve that they should not consume from the tree of good and evil but will, otherwise, have the freedom of abundance among all else.
P0065 The Devil, disguised as the Serpent, persuades Adam and Eve to eat from the tree. Both become filled with shame and blame the Serpent for tricking them. God curses all three and they fall from paradise into the burdens of mortality.
P0066 The allegory is describing an axiom of the persona which relates directly to the differentation between life and death, God and the Devil. This axiom applies to both the relationship to the self and the relationship to others, particularly in the relationship of partnership.
P0067 God creates a Prophet in response to neglect. The relationship between God, the Devil, the Serpent, and the Prophet is one which brings a message of warning about abundance, scarcity, burdens, and neglect.
P0068 The Serpent is like a rebellious child which tempts us away from abundance and guides us towards the Devil. The Prophet is like a bad parent which exposes us for our vulnerabilities and guides us back to God.
P0069 Adam and Eve are both parents and children, not to each other, but to the paradise which God created for them to protect.
P0070 The knowledge of good and evil serves to distract Adam and Eve from fulfilling their missions and obstructs them from obtaining the tree of life.
P0071 In Adam and Eve's transformation, they transition between Saints and Savages to themselves and each other. This process of transformation occurs, first, in the persona, and then in the world and our relationships with others. The Savages are unable to compromise or cooperate with one another and both blame the Serpent for their own misgivings. The Saints direct attention to the Prophets while neglecting their own abilities to resolve matters.
P0072 It is only when both Adam and Eve accept a shared responsibility for both their mission and their fall that they are able to share in the tree of life together.
P0074 Each complex arises from the oppositions between the ego-syntonic and ego-dystonic archetypes. Ego-syntonic archetypes being the Innocent, the Saint, the Missionary, and God.
P0075 As each ego-syntonic archetype either accepts or rejects its unconscious counterpart, the ego becomes distorted by unconscious projections which can sabotage our ability to adapt or function in ways which benefit our intentions.
P0076 Each of the four oppositional complexes will have the following compositions:
P0077 Orientation: Both will be either Rational or Irrational
P0078 Directive: One will be Introverted and the other will be Extroverted.
P0079 Nature: They will both be of the same nature.
P0081 Hero Complex: The Saint and the Savage - Identity and Advancement
P0082 The opposition between the Saint and the Savage represents the divide between our beliefs about our strengths and our weaknesses. The manifestation of this complex begs for us to resolve the divide so that we can temper our self-image to more accurately reflect to ourselves what our capabilities truly are.
P0083 It is typical that we would tell ourselves, "I am a 'Saint', not a 'Savage'." We may use other terminology to describe such things, but the contrasts are certainly made in our minds. What is important to realize is that there are taboos among what we perceive as Savage that are important and do work to bring benefits to our lives. We may not appreciate everything that someone else has to offer but we should consider what is working for them and integrate appropriate taboos into our own methods. If we are overly focused on what we disagree with and how we feel they should be revised, we will be consumed by a paralysing conflict that ultimately sabotages the success for everyone. It is only by, first, accepting the contributions from the Savage that we can begin to integrate our other complexities and individualize ourselves. There is a caution that excessive depreciation of the Savage will rear itself in the loss of taboos which are vital to self-sustaining growth.
P0085 The Saint is the archetype that we own in our conscious self-image. When we're really young, and we look up to those who manifest the perspective that will become dominant in our own consciousness. We then seek to be like them and emulate them; becoming better and better at the process as we go on. We eventually master it, and thus are able to withdraw the projection; seeing ourselves as the "Saint" in this area.We feel negative emotions of our Saint being obstructed by things in the Savage. Then, we become stubborn about them. The Savage then defends the Ego. "you're obstructing me, so I'll oppose you". What is being brought into consciousness is the need for the Savage to become integrated. We are really just rejecting ourselves but projecting it onto others. This projection is particularly strong when the God has not yet been distinguished from the Missionary of the opposite sex. To take ownership of the Savage, we must soften the harsh attitude of the Prophet and allow the God to surface without rejection. At the same time, we need to be mindful of our significant others need to transform and take up some of the space which we have dominated and coveted. This means that a masculine ego must come to respect and appreciate the apparently masculine qualities of his partner while also embracing his own inferior feminine qualities. Likewise, a feminine ego must come to respect and appreciate the apparently feminine qualities of her partner while also embracing her own inferior masculine qualities.
P0087 Child Complex: The Innocent and the Serpent - Deference to the other
P0088 The opposition between the Child and the Serpent represents the divide between our beliefs about what burdens and relieves us. The manifestation of this complex begs for us to resolve the divide so that we can temper our efforts to more accurately reflect what we feel we should give and take from those around us.
P0089 The persona 'blooms' forth as a type of psychic armament which serves to provide for the child complex through its psychic interactions. While the child complex is eternally disregulated, the persona adapts to the self and others as a means to bring alignment to its function.
P0090 Shame is repressed and deferred by the child complex. The relationship of these archetypes is like the relationship between Eve and the Serpent in the Abrahamic tradition of mythos. Eve is initially respectful of her deference to God, but the Serpent persuades her to violate the charter of abundance which was offered by God. 
P0091 When stuck in overly-responsible, too serious mode, the Innocent is projected in the concept of the other “refusing to grow up.” It inflates itself with the aim to be full of wisdom and maturity, as equal to the identity of others. It tells us what we want to hear while placing the other in a double-binded paradox. Then it deflates to become like a submissive child. Where the Innocent tells us what we want to hear, the Serpent tells us what we don't want to hear! We feel "bound", and then, in a rebellious fashion, try to turn the tables by using it for deceiving, double-binding, or trapping others. “Individuation” occurs when the ego finally becomes bored with the cycle of seeing life through the same perspectives because they haven’t really solved the pains of life. The Serpent floods consciousness with paradoxes that have no solution within the framework the ego has established. Turning all of the past deceptions and traps onto the ego to finally face the truth of the self.
P0092 We see others as a bad child or binding us, but we're being a bad child or binding ourselves.
P0093 What is trying to be brought into consciousness is the information that we have kept out of our consciousness to cope with past traumas. What we have, previously, blamed on others or allowed others to take control of regardless of our own needs.
P0095 Parent Complex: The Missionary and the Prophet - Authority Over Others
P0096 The opposition between the Missionary and the Prophet represents the divide between our beliefs about what we contribute and what we receive. The manifestation of this complex begs for us to resolve the divide so that we can temper our expectations about others to more accurately reflect our benefits in a relationship. Often, the Prophet is speaking the failings of their own innocence and mission but projecting it onto the innocence and missions of others.
P0097 The realization of the Missionary is often impeded by the defenses of the Innocent. When we do take ownership of the complex, we gain a strong motivation to teach and mentor others. During extremes, the Missionary can begin to preach the opposing attitude of the Prophet which can continue to inflation of the Innocent. The authoritarian figure of the Prophet feels neglected and becomes disgruntled, projecting what is actually our own internal criticism and neglect onto others. We take ownership of this by relaxing our criticisms and taking “fate” into our own hands. When we are no longer powerless to ourselves, we stop feeling powerless to others.
P0098 Often, it becomes the case that the Parent and Child can become embridled in conflict as the Prophet focuses on the Child and the Parent focuses on the Serpent. The solution is always that by directing the attention of the Prophet towards the Serpent and the attention of the Parent towards the Child, conflicts can be resolved and learning can occur. 
P0099 Spirit Complex: The God and the Devil - Connection to Life and Death; Aspirations, and the Shadow
P0100 The opposition between the God and the Devil represents the divide between our ideals and reality. The manifestation of this complex begs for us to resolve the divide so that we can temper our ideas and actions to respond accordingly to ourselves and others.
P0101 This complex is the most difficult to realize and should not be realized until the other complexes have been integrated.
P0103 The God is the collecting place of our sense of otherness. It is projected onto those we fall in love with to encase our feelings of inferiority with the opposite sex. There is a deep down longing for what they are brushing off. Unconsciously, we feel we would best be completed by someone who fits this ideal companion so we just find fault with people who use the Devilic attitude. We project all of our fears, shoulds, and negativities into the other by thinking of them as what they should do. We see others as completing us, but we need to see this completeness in ourselves. We need to become better at what we feel inferior at ourselves, rather than placing demands on others.
P0104 There is also a whole sense of "libido" or "life-giving energy" we tend to project onto the opposite sex. When we come to see this in ourselves, we will withdraw the projections, and also again gain more access to the unconscious. The God then becomes a "Prophet", and ultimately, an inner source of wisdom. What is trying to be brought into consciousness is the need to own the shadow; what is "not I", the ego-dystonic; and a good place to start is with converting the Savage into a figure of light rather than a dark projection of the Saint.
P0105 The Devil is the most suppressed area of consciousness. It carries all of the emotions associated with death. The destructive evil is really inside of ourselves. What is trying to be brought into consciousness is what the ego is fighting most against; the Savage, but in its sacred preferred orientation. In severe trauma, the Self uses it to try to keep information out of consciousness to protect the ego from more damaging content; and Devilic figures might appear in dreams threatening the ego with death if the ego's already fragile boundaries are being breached.
P0106 Owning this complex might make our ego feel it is in danger of becoming nonexistent, or transformed into the weak, pathetic, helpless image I mentioned earlier, that we despise as totally allowing the evil that opposed the ego to prevail. If the anima is the soul, then this represents the ego, rather than united with the soul, instead sapped of all life.
P0107 However, this figure, like the others, is really the ego's own projection. In actuality, the Devil is "replete with knowledge", "divine power," "fate" or "god." The intermediary spirits between us and the gods.
P0108 A single archetype which gets projected onto our enemies. The God, being the “bridge” to the Shadow, is the all-encompassing god of the psyche and our perspective of reality itself.

P0133 Complexes in the Persona
P0134 The persona is a regulatory function of the psyche which manages the interactions between the self or our collection of conscious and unconscious thoughts, feelings, ideas, awareness, etc., which is the subject, and the object, which is anything which could be considered physical. The subject is our mind and the object is our brain and everything which can interact with the brain by way of our nervous responses.
P0135 Clarification of Psychological Type Diagnosis
P0137 Psyche: The psyche is not a homogenous unit. Instead, it is an amalgam of contradictory impulses, inhibitions, and affects. To explain the psyche from without, it must be described as a system of adaptations, as it is done with any object of science.
P0139 Subject: The superordinate personality of the psyche including both the conscious and unconscious aspects of the psyche.
P0140 Object: An idea or image which the subject relates to with either real or magical value.
P0142 Extroversion: To devalue the subject and strip it of all irrelevant components while accommodating the object with empathy. An orientation which strives for the richest and most objective understanding of incoming phenomenon. 
P0143 Introversion: To devalue the object and strip it of all irrelevant components while accommodating the subject with abstraction. The various factors and preconditions which frame our psyche using past experiences and inner ideation. An anticipatory response to awareness.
P0145 Rational: A psychological function that is decisively influenced by reflection rather than perception. In accord with reason.
P0146 Feeling: An emotion is an affect resulting from the activation of a complex. Feeling differs from emotion in that it produces no perceptible physical innervations. It is a directive qualification of the value of either the subject or the object without significant regards to its composition.
P0147 Subjective Feeling - A regulation according to the quality of the subject. In its complexity, the value of the subject is depreciated. This manifests as a dependency or inability to be alone. The maladaptive expression is dependent personality disorder.
P0148 Objective Feeling - A regulation according to the quality of the object. In its complexity, the value of the object is depreciated. This manifests as a dissociation or involuntary escape from reality. The maladaptive expression is histrionic personality disorder.
P0149 Thinking: The directive quantification of the composition of either the subject or the object without significant regards to its value. 
P0150 Subjective Thinking - A regulation according to the composition of the subject. In its complexity, the value of the subject is depreciated. This manifests as an avoidance or isolation. The maladaptive expression is avoidant personality disorder.
P0151 Objective Thinking - A regulation according to the composition of the object. In its complexity, the value of the object is depreciated. This manifests as a paranoia or detachment. The maladaptive expression is schizoid personality disorder.
P0153 Irrational: A psychological function that is empirically influenced by perception rather than reflection. Such functions are absolutely contingent to reacting without any rational direction. Irrational functions monitor the existential facts about the object or the self.
P0154 Sensation: A perception via conscious reality which possesses extrinsic certainty and conviction.
P0155 Introverted Sensation - A regulation according to depreciation of the conscious experience. In its complexity, Si manifests as a compulsion or obsession.  
P0156 Extroverted Sensation - A regulation according to the appreciation of the conscious experience. 
P0157 Intuition: A perception via unconscious reality which possesses intrinsic certainty and conviction.
P0158 Introverted Intuition - A regulation according to the depreciation of the unconscious experience.
P0159 Extroverted Intuition - A regulation according to the appreciation of the unconscious experience.
P0161 Idealized: That which is not sufficiently realized is aggrandized in ideas or images of either light or dark.
P0162 Realized: That which is sufficiently realized is tempered in facts that are either sensible or insensible.

P0626 On the Integration of Duality
P0627 Each light projection towards the Other casts a dark projection upon the Self and each light projection towards the Self casts a dark projection towards the Other. These projections are manifestations of the locus of control.
P0628 When the locus of control is orientated externally, its projections are cast outwards to the Other as expectations or demands of what they should or should not be. When the locus of control is orientated internally, its projections are cast inward to the Self in the same way.
P0629 The manifestation of the opposition between God and Devil is the crux of the locus. These projections of the unconscious Ego are given external locus. In both projections, the essence of the projection is that the Other will do for the Self what the Self can(will) not do. The duality of each of these projections manifests in the Ego as the Saint and the Savage. While the Saint is typically of an internal locus, the Savage is the projection of its external locus. P0634 The relationship between the Saint and the Devil is that of a hunter to the beast, seeking to subdue and consume it for sustenance. It is an attitude of greed and vicariousness which fattens the hunter while his enemies, the Savage, starve. The Saintic representation of the Devil in the other bears the mark of vice. Both Saint and Devil consume one another while both God and Innocent are deprived. The Savage, which typically rises up against the Saint’s gluttony, is projected outward towards any who appear the threaten the egoism which ensues from such a relationship. In reality, it is not so graphic. The Devilic other is seen as a resource which enlightens us to a greater understanding and the symmetrical exchanges appear to be sophisticated confirmation of maturity and growth. In reality, it is a cycle of sophistry where the other Devil depreciates the God and projects its own fatalistic attitudes into the Missionary. The Devil appears as an angel of light who will guide us to truth. As the God depreciates, the other Savage becomes more threatening while the unconscious starvation of the Shadow is manifested in an external locus of control. The personal Devil becomes fatalistic and defers to the other Devil as a surrogate for both the Missionary and the God. The ego is no longer self-affirmed and the Serpent inflates to provide a false image to the libido. The dissonance of this image protects the Innocent which is becoming consumed by the Shadows and begins to project that fear into the Prophet who becomes a rigid authoritarian against the perceived Savage while the Tricker floods the mind with threatening images of what harm might come if the Savage is not suppressed. All of this occurs regardless of what the Ego actually aspires to accomplish. While attempts are made to rekindle the God, the Devil immediately consumes the light and beats it back into the Shadows.
P0635 This is why the Other God enters the life of the Saint. It drives away the Other Devils and gives space and support to the God so that it can manifest its true aspirations. The God projects onto this other, the idealization of its own aspirations. The Saint is a selfish persona, though. Its priority is manifested in the vanity of the ego and does not actually save anyone except for itself. It is merely the consequence of synchronicities that the Other God is trapped by the Other Savage being hunted. Likewise, the confidence of the Saint bears a resemblance to the Other God’s aspirational imagery. If the Other God’s Innocent calls forth the feelings of the Eternal in the Saint’s Missionary and the Saint reciprocates, then the Gods of both inflate their projections of the Shadow as the Idol God or Idol Goddess. Of course, these images cannot sustain in the frictions of reality and eventually fail. This makes the God duality appear as though it is less sustainable than those other relationships which have persisted through reality. The ego was not actually served and that draws forth feelings of betrayal and a projection of the Devil on the lovers. It appears as though it were novelty and the God shrinks back within, ashamed that they allowed themselves to be fooled by the allure. The truth of the God is that it does not want the Saint or the Other Saint to consume it. It wants to grow and develop into the light but it is afraid that the world will not accept it because it carries the sophistry of the Other Devil telling it that it is crude and under-developed. What the Other God is actually capable of is standing in juxtaposition to the God to modify the Missionaryal and Innocent energies so that the God can finally emerge with self determination, an internal locus of control. Once internal control of the God is obtained, the Devil can become differentiated and then integrated with the internal locus as well. It is only then that the Innocent becomes the Saint it has always imagined becoming and no longer need to be compensated for by the Devil or the Serpent.
P0644 Eventually, the encounter with the dual type will bring about the approach to individuation. Partners will seem fated to either part or destroy each other as they are each compelled to realize the true nature of their own Shadow within each other. The instinct is for the Serpent to emerge and fuels the Devil with fatalistic images of being controlled or limited. While one type strives for togetherness, the other type becomes repulsed. A bitter conflict ensues where each is defeating themselves but blaming the other for the transgressions against their greatest vulnerabilities. Cooperation becomes impossible as each becomes befuddled by the Serpent and begins to believe that the other is manipulating their God to become a twisted mess. In reality, the pain which is carried by the Serpent has accumulated in the inferiority of the God over the course of a lifetime. Ambitions and aspirations have been crushed by the invisible tyranny of their own Saint and Savage. 
P0645 The complexities of the unconscious do not merely project the darkness that we reject in ourselves but also what we aspire to become. We project the ideal self, which is contained within the God, into our affectionate other and habitually develop a vicarious obsession with the ideal that they, nor anyone else including ourselves, can actually live up to. We also project the abominable self, contained within the Devil which is, unfortunately quite attainable for some. At first, the dual type is like a god and then they are like a devil. The focus on the projected God shifts to focus on the projected Savage and the betrayal of the ideal becomes manifested in the self. Both duals appear to each other as though they have taken advantage and failed to reciprocate the proper support by never having met the idealized projection. The instinct is to look upon the other with disdain. From the subjective perspective, each may appear as malicious or narcissistic as the positive projections of the God are replaced by the negative projections of the Devil. Both the idealized and the depreciated projections of the God are the most grandiose archetypes of the ego. Our true and final self is caged between the aspects, longing to be freed from the turmoil.
P0646 The negative Devil that is projected onto the Dual is not without its merits. Though, generally, since the Shadow is an inferior projection, those projections represent our addictions and dependencies which are the vices limiting the realization of our personal aspirations.In actuality, the dual is not needed at all in the ways that the God or the Devil projects but they are the best suited to aid the other in obtaining a full realization of those aspirations in a realistic and grounded fashion. Unfortunately, with the God steeped in the impossible positive expectations of the opposite gender and the Devil steeped in the impossible negative expectations of the same gender, it is a rare occurrence that any such pairing achieves the level of synchronicity needed to grasp ownership of the Shadow and reach a conscious acceptance of the Devilic possession in a positive form. Should such a thing occur, the resulting controlled expression of the Devil is the final aspect of the transformative process from the mask of the Persona to the individuation of the conscious Self.
