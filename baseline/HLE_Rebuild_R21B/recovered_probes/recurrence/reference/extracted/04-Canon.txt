Revision note and abstract

The original five-page Canon of Permanent Truth (IDEA) provides the conceptual sequence and the
perspectives Self, Relation, Object, and System. The 28-page v2 attempts to derive that architecture
and universal truth from recursion. This corrected edition retains the architecture, repairs the formulas
throughout, and revises the conclusions that depended on invalid mathematics.
The changes are substantive. In particular, v2’s definition of recursive functions actually describes
finite-order permutations; its phase hierarchy changes mathematical types; its unrestricted fixed-point
condition is empty on every state space with at least two elements; and its final theorem uses non-return
where it requires return. Several examples also confuse truth, consistency, recurrence, and evidence.


Abstract

IDEA is presented here as a parameterized framework for testing whether a specified identity survives
specified transformations and returns. INITIATE records distinctions; DEFINE states structural con-
straints and the identity to be tracked; ENGAGE evaluates declared tests; ALIGN establishes invariance
under a declared family of return protocols and their finite compositions. The four perspectives organize
these tests without being asserted to follow uniquely from recursion.
A common state space keeps all operations well typed. A feature map makes preservation of identity
explicit. Return protocols are specified independently of their success, and universal claims are restricted
to the declared protocol family. A closure theorem proves that invariance under generating return maps
extends to every finite composite when those maps respect the chosen identity equivalence. Finite ter-
mination is established only under a finite-state assumption. The sixteen original examples are retained
with corrected logical and evidential assessments.


How to read this edition

• Definitions specify the framework. They do not prove that it is necessary or uniquely correct.
• Assumptions describe what an application must supply and what a theorem requires.
• Propositions and theorems include arguments establishing their stated conclusions.
• Interpretive passages preserve IDEA’s conceptual intent without treating it as a demonstrated
  universal ontology.

In this edition, permanence means preservation of a declared identity under every finite composition of declared
return protocols. It does not mean empirical certainty, indestructibility under arbitrary interventions, or truth
in every possible formal system.

1. Mathematical setting and notation

Let U be a set with at least two elements, used to represent a common background of possible distinctions.
Calling it a primordial field is an interpretation. A set already comes with membership and equality; set
notation does not derive distinction from a literally structureless origin.
Let X be a nonempty set of candidate states or structured descriptions. It is separate from the set
of transformations on those states. If a model contains persons, objects, relations, and systems, their
encodings must be distinguished within X, for example by type tags and context records.
For a set S, write P(S) for its power set. Let

                                       End(X) = {f | f : X → X}.

These are endofunctions, not necessarily computable, invertible, periodic, or successful at preserving
identity. All compositions below use the same X.

U           Background set for contrast witnesses.

The model uses ordinary classical mathematics. Applying IDEA to a different logic requires an explicit

2. Four perspectives as an explicit architecture

Retain the two descriptive axes interior/exterior and singular/plural. Their product supplies four labels:

                               Q = {interior, exterior} × {singular, plural}.

Use q0 = I, q1 = W e, q2 = It, and q3 = Its with the following correspondence.


Label    Perspective          Interpretive role
I        Interior, singular   Self: the represented standpoint or internal organization of one
                              participant.
We       Interior, plural     Relation: shared interpretation, coordination, and mutual expectations.
It       Exterior, singular   Object: the observable behavior or structure of an individual entity.
Its      Exterior, plural     System: interactions, constraints, and organization across a composite.


The count |Q| = 2 · 2 = 4 follows once these two binary axes are chosen. The choice and completeness
of those axes do not follow from the return equations.
The four proposed return motifs in v2 are useful descriptions but are not disjoint mathematical classes.
If r(a) = b and r(b) = a, that mutual return is also a two-step cycle. Taking f = r gives r(f (a)) = a,
so it is simultaneously an instance of transformative return. A map can also have fixed points elsewhere.
Fixed-point behavior alone therefore does not assign every map to exactly one of the four labels.
Here, each perspective receives an explicitly specified family of tests. A test may belong to more than one
perspective. Thus HI , HW e , HIt , HIts may overlap; they are not asserted to partition all transformations.
Labels are not state spaces, and expressions such as Rec(W e) are not used.


What a fourfold derivation would still require

A derivation would need independently stated primitives, an equivalence relation on candidate phases
or scopes, a rule of generation, a proof of exhaustiveness, and a proof that no smaller collection suﬀices.
None of these conclusions follows simply by naming four return patterns. This edition uses the fourfold
organization as a framework assumption, preserving it as a subject for later investigation.

3. Return protocols and the identity being tested

Choose a feature map κ : X → K. It states what is to count as the identity of a candidate during this
assessment:
                                  x ≡κ y ⇐⇒ κ(x) = κ(y).                                          (1)
This is an equivalence relation because equality in K is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Exact-state
return is the special case K = X and κ = idX .
For each q ∈ Q, supply a nonempty family

                                        Pq ⊆ End(X) × End(X).

A pair p = (f, r) describes a transformation f followed by a candidate return r. Its completed map is
hp = r ◦ f . Define                                              ∪

                            κ(r(f (x))) = κ(x),      equivalently hp (x) ≡κ x.                          (3)

The input may change: f (x) 6= x is compatible with a successful return. That inequality is required
only when a particular test is intended to be a nontrivial perturbation. Neither every phase nor every

Requirements that prevent a circular test

The state space, features, protocol maps, and application conditions must be specified before certifying
success. A failed protocol remains a failed protocol; it is not removed by defining “admissible” to mean
“successful.” A return map cannot be chosen after inspecting the answer merely to reset that particular
candidate.
If recovery uses a stored copy, a memory, another participant, or a resource, that information and its
cost belong in the state and protocol. Operations that fail to apply must be represented as failures or
explicitly limited to a stated domain, not silently omitted from a universal claim. This edition uses total
maps on an adequate state space for simplicity.
The identity features should also discriminate the alternatives relevant to the claim. A constant κ makes
all states equivalent and every return pass. That is a mathematically valid but uninformative assessment.

4. Periodicity, fixed points, and recovery are different

The original definition of Rec(X) is repaired by renaming it:

                          FinOrd(X) = {r ∈ End(X) : ∃k ∈ N>0 , rk = idX }.                          (4)

The condition describes finite-order permutations, not recursive functions in computability theory. The
exponent must be positive: allowing k = 0 would make every endofunction qualify, since r0 = idX .

Proposition 1. If rk = idX for some positive integer k, then r is bijective and r−1 = rk−1 .

Proof. Both r ◦ rk−1 and rk−1 ◦ r equal rk = idX . Thus rk−1 is a two-sided inverse.

The condition ∃k ∀x rk (x) = x also imposes one common period. It is stronger, on an infinite set, than
∀x ∃kx rkx (x) = x.

Proposition 2. If |X| ≥ 2, no point is fixed by every member of FinOrd(X).

Proof. Given x ∈ X, choose y 6= x. The transposition exchanging x and y and fixing every other point
has order two and moves x. Therefore
                                          ∩
                                                  Fix(r) = ∅.
                                        r∈FinOrd(X)




This is why ALIGN must use a declared family of operations instead of every possible reversible map.
It is also why r(x) = x cannot replace r(f (x)) = x: the former asks whether the return operation alone
fixes a state, while the latter tests a completed transformation-and-return protocol.
On an infinite set, finite-order permutations need not even be closed under composition. On Z, let
a(n) = −n and b(n) = 1 − n. Then a2 = b2 = id, but (a ◦ b)(n) = n − 1 has infinite order. Closure under
composition must therefore be constructed separately.

I. INITIATE: make the contrast explicit

     The first distinction is the beginning of an identity question: what differs, and what is expected
     to remain recognizable through change?


Define the contrast space by
                                      ∆ = {(a, b) ∈ U × U : a 6= b}.                                      (5)
This defines distinguishable pairs; it does not prove that a pair is minimal. A notion of minimality would
require an additional order, complexity measure, or decomposition rule.
The requirement in v2 that some finite-order map sends a to b adds nothing if all such maps are al-
lowed: the transposition of any unequal pair already does so. If only some transitions are physically or
operationally available, their availability is extra model data.
For each perspective, choose a contrast-witness map cq : X → U × U . Set

                                        Aq (x)   ⇐⇒      cq (x) ∈ ∆.                                      (6)

If cq (x) = (aq (x), bq (x)), this means aq (x) 6= bq (x). Membership of both terms in U gives their common
formal background. A causal or historical common origin would require a further relation; it is not
established by set membership.
The INITIATE filter acts on a set S ⊆ X:

                                    ∇(S) = {x ∈ S : ∀q ∈ Q, Aq (x)}.                                      (7)

It selects candidates with recorded distinctions in every required perspective. Candidate generation,
if needed, is a separate operation. The filter does not manufacture U , equality, or information from
nothing.


Four dimensions of initiation

For Self, identify the participant’s relevant distinction. For Relation, identify the difference that co-
ordination must span. For Object, identify observable alternatives. For System, identify distinct
components, states, or arrangements.
These requirements are operational choices for an application of IDEA. A missing witness means that
initiation is not established under that application. It does not prove that the candidate is meaningless,
false, or nonexistent.

II. DEFINE: state the structure and its identity

     Definition makes explicit what the contrast is a contrast of, and what a successful return must
     preserve.


For each perspective, supply a structural predicate Dq : X → {true, false}. It can check a stated grammar,
a relation among components, a conservation constraint, or a consistency requirement. Its content must
be given in the application; the word “coherent” alone is not a test.
Using the identity criterion from Equation (1), define

                                 B = {x ∈ X : ∀q ∈ Q, Aq (x) ∧ Dq (x)},                                (8)

and the DEFINE filter
                                       µ(S) = S ∩ B      (S ⊆ X).                                      (9)
Thus µ(∇(S)) retains initiated candidates satisfying the declared structural requirements. It does not
assume that all future tests will succeed; that question belongs to ENGAGE and ALIGN.
The formula in v2 requiring x 6= y for all x, y ∈ ∆ is removed. Substituting y = x makes that requirement
contradictory whenever ∆ is nonempty. Contrast belongs to the two members of a designated pair, not
to every pair of variables regardless of whether they name the same object.


Four dimensions of definition

Self: specify which internal organization or commitment is being tracked. Relation: state the shared
expectations or compatibility conditions. Object: specify the relevant observable form. System: state
the relations among components that constitute the organized whole.
Cogence, coherence, cohesion, and congruence remain useful names for questions about understanding,
consistency, integration, and correspondence. They become mathematical conditions only when supplied
with explicit predicates or measurements.


Identity may be revised, but the claim then changes

The feature map κ may be learned or revised between assessments. Within one theorem or test report
it is fixed. Changing it to hide a failed return creates a different assessment; it does not retroactively
establish the earlier one. A model of learning may represent changing criteria in its state, but it must
then specify the higher-level identity it is evaluating.

III. ENGAGE: test declared transformations and returns

       To engage is to expose a proposed identity to a test whose outcome can matter.


For every perspective, choose a nonempty test family Eq ⊆ Pq . Define

                             Eq (x)   ⇐⇒    ∀(f, r) ∈ Eq , κ(r(f (x))) = κ(x).                       (10)

Then
                                  ε(S) = {x ∈ S ∩ B : ∀q ∈ Q, Eq (x)}.                               (11)
Each result concerns all tests in the stated engagement family, rather than merely the existence of one
convenient pair of maps. Choosing only f = r = idX would make a return test automatic and provide
no evidence of robustness under change.
A test is evaluated only when its required predicate has been checked or proved. If its outcome is
unavailable, record “unassessed”; do not count an empty record as a pass. The mathematical predicate
has a definite interpretation in a fully specified model, while our evidence about it may be incomplete.


Four dimensions of engagement

Self: test whether the tracked internal organization remains recognizable after a challenge. Relation:
test whether coordination or mutual expectations can be restored after a specified divergence. Object:
test form or behavior under a declared intervention. System: test the organization of a composite after
an alteration to its parts or connections.
Neither success nor failure carries an automatic metaphysical verdict. A failed return can expose a poor
definition, an inadequate recovery procedure, missing information, a genuine boundary of stability, or an
incorrect prediction. The observed failure is evidence about the stated model and claim.


The quantifiers must match the claim

The statement ∀f ∃r describes recoverability with an available choice of recovery; ∃r ∀f requires one
recovery that works for every transformation. These are different assertions. This edition evaluates
specified pairs (f, r) and universally quantifies over them. An adaptive recovery policy is permitted
when its inputs, memory, and rule are explicitly encoded in the protocol.

     Alignment is identity that survives every return required by the stated assessment.


Let M = hHi be the monoid of all finite compositions of the completed maps in H, including the empty
composition idX . Define
                            I∗ = {x ∈ B : ∀h ∈ M, κ(h(x)) = κ(x)}.                               (12)
This is the set of aligned candidates. It may be empty. Its members preserve the declared identity under
every finite sequence of completed protocols, including sequences that mix perspectives.

Thus a fixed-point expression is available without demanding that a state resist every imaginable opera-

What the condition does and does not preserve

Equation (12) checks identity at the ends of completed protocols. It does not require every intermediate

remains relative to the definition and protocol family.


A finite test is not an unrestricted guarantee

Passing the engagement subset Eq does not establish all of Pq , unless those families coincide or a proof
covers the remainder. Likewise, invariance under every finite composite does not assert convergence of
an infinite trajectory. A limit claim needs additional topology, dynamics, and hypotheses.

Record evidence rather than filling cells by analogy

For a particular x, a cell can be established, failed, or unassessed. These are evidential statuses,

9. Worked mathematical examples

A. Exact recovery after nontrivial change

Let X = {0, 1, 2} and κ = idX . In each perspective, use the protocol (f, r) where f exchanges 0 and 1
and fixes 2, and r = f . Choose U = {u, v}, cq (x) = (u, v), and Dq (x) = true for all x, q, so B = X. Let
Eq = Pq = {(f, r)}.
                                    r ◦ f = f 2 = idX ,      I∗ = X.
The states 0 and 1 move during the transformation but return exactly. State 2 is unchanged. This
demonstrates the mathematical distinction between change and successful return. Repeating one protocol
in four perspectives is formally permitted but supplies no evidence that the perspectives are independent.


B. A protocol that separates success from failure

Keep the same X, κ, and B. In one perspective add the pair (d, idX ), with d(0) = 0, d(1) = 0, and
d(2) = 2. Then
                               Fix(d) = {0, 2},    I∗ = {0, 2}.
State 1 fails this additional protocol. More strongly, no single map r : X → X can exactly recover both
0 and 1 from d, because d(0) = d(1) would require r(0) = 0 and r(0) = 1 simultaneously. Recovery of
the lost distinction requires extra information or a weaker identity criterion.


C. Preserving a feature while the state changes

Let X = {0, 1, 2, 3}, κ(x) = x mod 2, and h(x) = x + 2 (mod 4). The map h has no exact fixed points,
but
                                   κ(h(x)) = κ(x) for every x ∈ X.
It respects the parity equivalence. Thus, with B = X and H = {h}, every state is aligned relative to
parity and none is fixed under the nonidentity generator as an exact state. The result is specifically
about parity; it does not show that every property of the state is preserved.


What these examples establish

The repaired formalism admits nonempty results, meaningful failures, exact recovery, and feature preser-
vation. Those examples establish the behavior of the model. They do not prove that invariance is
identical to semantic truth.

10. Corrected example corpus: cases 1-4

The original sixteen cases are retained. Their unsupported checkmark grids are replaced by explicit
logical assessments. Applying the formal grid would additionally require X, κ, the predicates, and the
protocol families for each case.


1. The liar paradox

Claim: “This statement is false.” With an unrestricted classical truth predicate, the intended self-
reference leads to a truth value ℓ satisfying
                                              ℓ = ¬ℓ.
No Boolean value satisfies this equation. Calling the liar simply false is not a solution: on its intended
reading, its falsity would make what it says true.
Corrected status: The unrestricted classical truth assignment is inconsistent; the sentence is not
thereby proved “permanently false.” Notice that negation on {0, 1} has period two. A cycle of truth-
value changes can return, yet fail to solve ℓ = ¬ℓ. Recurrence alone does not resolve the paradox.


2. Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem

Claim: “This statement is unprovable in this system.” Fix an effectively axiomatized theory T extending
suﬀicient arithmetic. A Gödel sentence is constructed to satisfy
                                       T ` GT ↔ ¬ ProvT (⌜GT ⌝).
If T is sound for the standard natural numbers, then GT is true there and unprovable in T . The Gödel-
Rosser theorem obtains incompleteness from consistency alone, using a modified sentence. The theory,
encoding, and hypotheses matter; a bare English self-reference is not the theorem. [1, Chapter 5]
Corrected status: A theorem about formal provability under stated assumptions. IDEA does not
independently certify GT or escape the theorem’s limitations.


3. “There is no truth”

If asserted as true and intended to include that very assertion, the unrestricted claim defeats itself under
ordinary classical semantics. A restricted claim such as “none of these particular reports is true” need
not do so.
Corrected status: The unrestricted self-inclusive assertion is self-undermining. Its failure does not
prove IDEA’s particular phases, scopes, or universal necessity.


4. Identity tautology: “A is A”

For an object denoted by a, a = a follows from the semantics of equality. For a proposition P , P → P
is a propositional tautology. These are different grammatical uses of “A.”
Corrected status: Valid under the stated logical interpretation. The result does not require, and does
not prove, a derivation of identity from recursive dynamics.

5. Contradiction: “A is not A”

With ordinary equality and a fixed referent, a 6= a is false in every interpretation. If the intended formula
is instead P ∧ ¬P , it is unsatisfiable in classical propositional logic. Different times, respects, referents,
or logics must not be silently identified.
Corrected status: Classically false or unsatisfiable under the precise reading above. Failure is estab-
lished by the logical semantics, not by a missing return path.


6. Self-reproducing function

“This function outputs itself” must distinguish a program from its encoded description. For a program-
text string p, a quine satisfies
                                              Run(p) = p,
where Run means execution under a specified interpreter and the output is compared as text. A function
returning a reference to itself is a different construction.
Corrected status: A particular self-reproduction claim can be established by a construction or execu-
tion trace. It does not make the program, all its outputs, or all claims about it universally true. The
original example supplied no program to verify.


7. “Aliens exist”

This is an empirical existence claim whose meaning depends on such matters as what counts as life and
the intended range of places and times. The absence of a completed IDEA assessment supplies neither
a proof nor a disproof.
Corrected status: Unresolved by the formalism alone. Evidence is required; lack of a return certificate
does not establish falsehood or inherent untestability.


8. “Identity emerges from recursion”

Within the corrected model, a stable identity relative to κ and M is defined by Equation (12). An
application can investigate how patterns meeting that definition form or persist.
That does not derive the equality relation used to define sets and maps. Nor does it establish that all
physical, personal, or semantic identity has the same origin. A causal account of the emergence of a
pattern needs dynamics and evidence beyond a definition of its invariance.
Corrected status: A legitimate modeling definition and a broader research hypothesis, depending on
the reading. The universal ontological claim remains unproved.

9. There is no largest prime

Assume that p1 , . . . , pn are all the primes. Set

                                              N = p1 p2 · · · pn + 1.

Since N > 1, it has a prime divisor p. For every i, division of N by pi leaves remainder 1, so p is absent
from the list. This is a contradiction. The number N itself need not be prime.
Corrected status: A proved mathematical theorem. Its proof supplies the justification; a four-by-four
pattern of checkmarks would not replace that proof.


10. Law of non-contradiction

In classical propositional logic,
                                                      ¬(P ∧ ¬P )
is true for both Boolean values of P . “Nothing can both be and not be” needs that same proposition,
interpretation, time, and respect to be meaningful as this logical law.
Corrected status: Classically valid. Other logical systems require their own semantics; a claim about
all such systems does not follow from the classical truth table.


11. Digital simulation hypothesis

“We live in a simulation” leaves the simulator, implementation, and empirical consequences unspecified.
Particular simulation proposals might have observable consequences; direct access to a simulator is not
logically necessary for every possible test.
Corrected status: The broad claim is underdetermined here. It is not shown false by a supposed
failure of “systemic return,” and no formal IDEA certificate has been supplied.


12. Axiom of Choice

For every set-indexed family (Ai )i∈J of nonempty sets, the Axiom of Choice asserts that there is a
function c with domain J such that

                                          c(i) ∈ Ai      for every i ∈ J.

It is an axiom of ZFC and is independent of ZF if ZF is consistent. Thus it holds in every model of ZFC;
IDEA has not proved it from weaker assumptions. [2] [3]
Corrected status: Assumed in ZFC; independent of ZF under the consistency condition. “Recursively
coherent” is not a substitute for specifying the background axioms.

13. Corrected example corpus: cases 13-16

13. Circular definition: “X is defined by X”

The equation x = x is satisfied by every x in its domain. If the domain has more than one element,
it does not determine a unique one. It is uninformative as a standalone definition, rather than false.
Recursive equations can define objects when their additional constraints establish existence and, where
needed, uniqueness.
Corrected status: An insuﬀicient definition as stated. Circular syntax by itself proves neither contra-
diction nor a successful construction.


14. Schrödinger’s cat and quantum measurement

An idealized entangled state has the form

                         |Ψi = α|alivei|Ea i + β|deadi|Ed i,   |α|2 + |β|2 = 1.

Decoherence suppresses interference in the reduced description as the environmental states become nearly
orthogonal. That does not, by itself, select a unique outcome or prove that the original state has been
recovered. Outcome claims require the relevant measurement framework or interpretation. [4, Sections
II and IV]
Corrected status: The original claim that this physically demonstrates IDEA’s permanent recursive
closure is unsupported. A superposition is also not the classical proposition P ∧ ¬P .


15. Modal collapse: “Everything that could happen must happen”

Read this as ♢P → □P . Consider two mutually accessible worlds u, v, with P false at u and true at v.
At u, ♢P is true and □P is false. The implication therefore fails in this model, even on an S5 frame.
Corrected status: Not valid in standard normal modal logics admitting that frame. It is not a
contradiction in every model: a one-world reflexive frame validates the schema. Modal possibility and
necessity must not be conflated.


16. Ontological grounding: “Existence exists”

The phrase needs an interpretation. If formalized as ∃x (x = x), it is valid in ordinary first-order
semantics with nonempty domains. If empty domains are permitted, it is false there.
Corrected status: A consequence of the chosen nonempty-domain semantics under that translation.
It does not independently prove a particular primordial field or a universal theory of existence.

14. Permanence, truth, and the limits of the result

IDEA can organize a disciplined investigation of a candidate’s identity through transformation. Its
mathematical result is a conditional invariance claim. Four other questions must remain distinct:


Question                              Required justification
Is a proposition true in a model?     An interpretation and its satisfaction relation.
Is it provable from a theory?         A derivation using the theory’s axioms and rules.
Does a state preserve its identity?   A specified identity criterion, operations, and an invariance
                                      argument.
Is an empirical claim supported?      Observations or experiments linked to the claim by an adequate
                                      model.


A representation of a false statement can remain perfectly stable under repeated copying. A true re-
port can change its physical encoding. Invariance of a representation is therefore neither suﬀicient nor
necessary for the truth of its content without additional assumptions connecting those notions.
If an application represents a formula φ inside x, the condition x ∈ I∗ does not by itself imply A |= φ
for an interpretation A. To obtain that implication, one must prove a soundness connection between the
assessment predicates and the intended semantics. Defining a predicate to mean “is true” supplies an
assumption or an oracle, not an independent method for deciding truth.


The universal claims retained as hypotheses

The philosophical proposal that contrast, definition, testing, and return are necessary for every intelligible
inquiry can be argued for and investigated. It is not established by the closure theorem. A critic’s use of
language or distinctions does not entail that the critic accepts IDEA’s specific architecture, and a failed
candidate does not automatically confirm the framework.
The former “unfalsifiability corollary” is therefore withdrawn as a mathematical result. Definitions are
not empirical predictions, but models and applications built with them can still be challenged by incorrect
predictions, unsuitable identity criteria, missing scopes, or failed returns. A return-based explanation
must make contact with those possible failures to earn evidential support.


     To initiate is to make a distinction. To define is to state what is being preserved. To engage
     is to expose that claim to a test. To align is to establish the return that the stated conditions
     actually support.

Appendix B. Additional checks and remaining obligations

A direct counterexample to the old phase derivation

Take U = {0, 1}. The old contrast set has two elements, (0, 1) and (1, 0). Its proposed first phase set
consists only of the transposition of those two contrasts. The next stage is empty, because a singleton
has no nonidentity permutation. Subsequent stages remain empty. The cardinalities are

                                              2, 1, 0, 0, . . .

That construction neither accumulates four phases nor proves that four mutually irreducible transfor-
mations exist. Its union also mixes contrast objects with functions on those objects. Merely repairing a
typographical sign would leave these defects intact.


Finite checks used in this revision

The small-state examples, the empty universal-fixed-point counterexample, the distinction between ex-
act and feature-based return, and the necessity of the equivalence-respecting hypothesis were checked
computationally. The generator theorem was also checked against all 378 unordered pairs (allowing
repetition) of the 27 maps on a three-element state space and all five equivalence relations. All 1,116
cases satisfying its hypothesis passed; 414 cases without that hypothesis showed why the restriction can
matter. These checks supplement the proofs; they do not replace them or establish an empirical claim
about the world.


What remains to be supplied in an actual application

1. Define U , X, and any encoding of types, context, memory, and resources.
2. Specify the contrast witnesses cq , structural predicates Dq , and identity features κ.
3. Declare the transformation-and-return pairs in each Pq , together with an engagement subset Eq and
   its evidence.
4. Prove Equation (17) if generator checks are to stand for every composite; otherwise assess the full
   monoid criterion.
5. State whether the result concerns exact recovery, feature preservation, an approximate metric, or a
   stronger condition on entire paths.
6. Provide any further argument connecting the return criterion to an empirical prediction or a semantic
   truth claim.

The corrected theorem creates a consistent formal target for these choices. It does not select them
uniquely. The proposed connection between IDEA and the necessary structure of all intelligibility remains
additional philosophical work.
