# HLE Id/Identity developmental coupling — executed probe v1

17 September 2026 · R11 runtime with an isolated experimental developmental controller

**Connecting recurrence to action consequences and embodiment produced useful retained learning in the declared small world.** The coupled controller passed its acquisition, retention/transfer, and comparative feedback criteria. With ample resources it handled every post-training volatile demand appropriately. An identical generic development loop matched its complete execution, so these results establish the feedback connection within the existing grammar, not unique Id/Identity semantics or an optimal 3:1 frequency.

## What changed from the earlier probe

The earlier experiment repeatedly read cue-bound memories and evaluated recall performance. This experiment makes each phase perform a real content transformation through R4:

| Experimental Identity phase | Real work | What becomes available later |
|---|---|---|
| Sun 19 | Contextual paid recall, then Theorize: I → ITS | An account derived from the selected memory propositions and any recalled checking capability. |
| Judgement 20 | Apply: ITS → IT | Paid inspections/transfers, private receipts, successes, failures and prediction discrepancies. |
| World 21 | Embody: IT → I, followed by paid exact cue rebinding | Revised remembered consequences and experience-derived checking capacity, accessible to the next circuit. |

An initial error can therefore change the next action. The controller never inserts a capability record or chooses the correct owner for the participant. R4's existing Embody operation creates its supplied `inspect_before_transfer` capability after discrepant experience. Later Theorize and Apply operations must actually retrieve that capacity before using it.

The fixed TIM and current active information element determine each real Model A route and its price. There is no free perspective reset. R5 independently assesses demand, path, retained capacity and candidate Shell patterns; its verdicts are not fed to participant decisions.

These phase meanings are **implementation hypotheses**. The Fool's Memory manual P0107–P0117 states the 3:1 recurrence while leaving the traversal and Id/Identity equivalence open. Developmental Dynamics P0328–P0359 motivates embodiment, retained capacity and renewed demand, but does not derive Sun = Theorize or the other assignments.

The clock also has a newly declared operational unit: one outer step is one completed developmental phase. Three phases complete one demand circuit; three circuits complete one nine-step outer lap. Twelve circuits complete four outer laps and twelve inner laps. The earlier probe used three read queries per outer step. Both are explicit hypotheses, and the earlier retrieval results remain valid for their own schedule.

Card rank, folded location, Model A seat and Crux perspective remain separate. The outer counter groups completed phases; it introduces no separate Ego or Super-Ego content transformation. This limits what can be concluded about the source's complete architecture.

## Declared evaluation

The fixed main panel crossed six TIMs (IEE, LSE, LSI, EII, ILE and ESI), three seeds, two demand regimes, two actor work budgets, and five policies: **360 trajectories**. An additional **32 controls** covered all sixteen TIMs with typed processing and fully neutral routing/prices.

Each run offered twelve demands over seven objects. The volatile regime included ownership changes that invalidate remembered self-ownership, renewed opportunities where transfer is valid, and previously unused objects. A ten-tick quiet interval followed circuit six. The always-executable regime made every offered transfer valid, testing the price of precaution where invalid transfers need not occur. Seeds permuted object identities; they did not randomize the demand pattern or supply independent stochastic samples.

The 600- and 2,400-unit budgets governed real, equal energy/time expenditure. Initial memory construction cost 42 units and was excluded equally. External ownership changes used actual R2 transfers. When an external fixture required a transfer charged to the participant, an explicit two-unit credit covered precisely that fixture action; the audit reconciled these credits separately. No automatic developmental replenishment was supplied.

| Policy | Recall and publication |
|---|---|
| Coupled | Recall the current episode plus the shared World21 lesson; publish that lesson after every circuit. |
| Episode only | Perform the same paid retention/publication, but recall only the current object's episode. |
| Feedback unavailable | Perform paid processing and retention, but subsequent recall uses the unchanged original cue. |
| Publish once per outer lap | Recall current/shared memories, but publish the shared lesson only every third circuit. |
| Generic coupled loop | Execute precisely the coupled operations and feedback under generic stage names. |

The middle controls isolate availability and timing of retained information. The feedback-unavailable control still pays to embody its outcomes, so it is not a claim that disabling learning entirely would have the same price.

## Main result: retained learning changes actions

With 2,400 units, all eighteen volatile conditions for each policy completed all twelve circuits. The first circuit deliberately exposed an invalid ownership belief. The following eleven demands supplied the post-training comparison.

| Policy | Appropriate post-training demands | Rate | Invalid transfers across all 216 demands, including training | Mean total work per run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Coupled | **198/198** | **100%** | 18 | 633.0 |
| Episode only | 126/198 | 63.6% | 90 | 529.0 |
| Feedback unavailable | 108/198 | 54.5% | 108 | 518.5 |
| Publish once per outer lap | 180/198 | 90.9% | 36 | 581.5 |
| Generic coupled loop | **198/198** | **100%** | 18 | 633.0 |

An appropriate demand required no invalid transfer and a successful transfer whenever the actor owned the object. A valid decision to inspect and withhold transfer when the actor did not own it counted as appropriate. A failed transfer remained a recorded failure even when later learning succeeded.

In all eighteen coupled conditions, the first failed transfer produced a capability through real Embody work. The second circuit involved a different object. Its initial remembered owner was still self, but the recalled capability caused an inspection that prevented another invalid transfer. The episode-only, feedback-unavailable and delayed-publication controls could not use that shared lesson at this early demand: **0/18 early cross-object guard uses**, versus **18/18** for the coupled controller.

The learned guard remained available after the quiet interval and on the final unseen object. R5 established retained checking use on both the original object and other objects in all eighteen ample volatile coupled runs. This is reuse within the existing ownership/checking grammar; no new action grammar or capability family was invented.

Compared with feedback unavailable, the coupled controller improved appropriate post-training behavior by **45.45 percentage points**, exceeding the declared 20-point threshold. Publishing every circuit also made the lesson available before the early new-object demand. The comparison supports that timing advantage in this specified demand sequence; it does not establish a generally optimal update frequency.

## Cost and scarcity

Coupling increased mean work from 518.5 to 633.0 units in the ample volatile panel: **22.1% more total work**, including recall, Model A processing, physical actions, embodiment and cue publication. The additional work produced fewer invalid actions; it was not a free improvement.

At a 600-unit limit, some types could not finish all twelve coupled circuits. The table keeps unfinished demands in the planned-demand denominator:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Coupled | 6/18 | 192/216 | 174/198 | 18 |
| Episode only | 12/18 | 210/216 | 126/198 | 84 |
| Feedback unavailable | 12/18 | 210/216 | 108/198 | 108 |
| Publish once per outer lap | 12/18 | 204/216 | 168/198 | 36 |

The coupled controller continued to avoid post-training invalid transfers on completed demands, but its additional work reduced completion. Actual action receipts were counted even if the run exhausted resources before embodying that action's outcome.

When every offered transfer was executable, all policies achieved 198/198 appropriate post-training demands with ample resources. Mean work was 644.5 units for coupling and 503.5 with feedback unavailable. At 600 units, only 6/18 coupled runs finished, compared with 18/18 feedback-unavailable runs. The experiment therefore does not support unconditional checking or a universal efficiency advantage.

The always-executable histories can still generate a prediction discrepancy when an object is returned after an earlier transfer. Their R5 retained-capacity criterion remained unassessed because the declared original-plus-new-object checking demonstration was not completed in the required form. That is not evidence that no capability was learned.

## What the developmental assessment establishes

All **784 final R5 study reports** returned `no_persistent_candidate` under the implemented detector and supplied conditions. No Shell clearance occurred or was claimed. The retained-capacity studies left general closure **unassessed in all 392 runs**. Learning a useful procedure does not establish a new developmental altitude or all finite-composition closure.

R5 retained prediction discrepancies and strict path-condition failures even when a guard prevented an invalid action. In the IEE coupled volatile example, the first circuit cost 56 units; later circuits cost 60–70. Type-route work dropped from 18 units initially to 15 in later circuits, while total costs also reflected recalled content, inspection and retention. These are actual paid path measurements. Extra work alone is not identified as compensatory Shell maintenance.

The positive finding is narrower and concrete: consequences were embodied, retained capacity became causally available through memory, and that capacity changed later actions under renewed and new-object demands.

## Type and Identity controls

All sixteen TIMs completed the ample coupled volatile route control. Costs ranged from **455 units (LSI) to 860 (EIE)** under the engine's declared price model; IEE used 780. The sufficiently funded outcome sequence was the same. With typed routing and positional prices both neutralized, all sixteen types had identical paths, outcomes and **312-unit** costs. This confirms that the probe actually exercised existing Model A routing and pricing, rather than resetting the actor or supplying type-specific answers. It does not calibrate human effort or rank psychological types.

Across **72 matched main-panel pairs**, the coupled and generic-coupled controllers produced identical engine-state hashes and episode records. The controller labels have no separate content semantics beyond the operations already assigned to them. Thus:

- Paid developmental feedback works in the declared bounded implementation.
- The selected 3:1 clock can organize that feedback consistently.
- Unique Id/Identity meanings, an independent role for the outer Ego/Super-Ego cycle, and superiority over other recurrence ratios remain open.
