Its complete lower repertoire is native action, repeated native action, sequential transfers, relabeling, use of the current cycle, cohort revision, and dropping departed-member duties. Physical endpoints, stable identities, exact authorization, retained shared procedure, inherited linkage and enacted duties are separate state components.

Both candidate constructors are enumerated before sufficiency is tested. `consent_cycle` creates the first retained shared relation. `carry_obligations` must reuse an available lower cycle and explicitly link inherited duties before they can be accepted. Without a lower constituent, that constructor cannot solve the demand. A reversed primitive sequence and a renamed wrapper also fail. A currently adequate retained rule yields horizontal adaptation. Incomplete search yields unassessed. No sufficient candidate yields unresolved.

Candidate order is Pareto order on added dependencies and duty-work extent; equally minimal alternatives are preserved by the selector. The actual case has one minimum at each boundary. Redundant-check variants are dominated. These are declared protocol costs; the report separately measures actual charged engine work.

This is exhaustive only within the seven lower programs and two constructors (plus their redundant-check variants). It is not an exhaustive search of all possible Python controllers, open-ended social organizations or all conceivable conductive strategies. The shared demand itself includes reusable exact consent and obligation continuity; it is richer than moving an object to an endpoint.

## Observer and participant boundaries

The `settle` operation is an external assessment boundary. It emits no participant observation and charges no participant wallet. Participants renew from retained, authenticated `SharedPlan` records and exact consents. They do not use evaluator depth, status or invalidation as decision inputs. A poisoned-evaluator control exercises this boundary.

Current claims and historical successes are distinct. The first cohort’s claim loses current validity when its founder leaves. Its unchanged lower algorithm and historical evidence remain accessible. The second claim requires new current successor consents and actual new performance. It also retains the consequential evidence establishing its lower closure; withdrawing that evidence invalidates the higher claim.

Child and origin dependencies are indexed. A changed child invalidates dependent parents; unrelated revisions do not. Current native capacity withdrawal, material suppression, supporting evidence withdrawal and reopened individual clearance invalidate the relevant shared claim. Re-exposing a historical record does not silently recertify an invalidated claim.
